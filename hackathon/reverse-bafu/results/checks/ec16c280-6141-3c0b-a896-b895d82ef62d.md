# Check: Polyols, at plant (ec16c280-6141-3c0b-a896-b895d82ef62d)

target `bafu-2026` vs explicit model `ec16c280-6141-3c0b-a896-b895d82ef62d-disagg` and hybrid `ec16c280-6141-3c0b-a896-b895d82ef62d-hybrid`

**Strategy:** S3 — top-down model: propylene oxide by the alkoxylation mass balance, utilities calibrated. NOT S1. No report in the bundle prints an inventory. The dataset's ecoSpold technology field is 'production out of alcohols and epoxides by alkoxylation'. Alkoxylation is an addition: the epoxide opens onto the growing chain with no mass loss, so epoxide + initiator alcohol = 1.000 kg per kg of polyol. The epoxide of a polyether polyol is propylene oxide (with some ethylene oxide in tipped grades), and BAFU carries 'Propylene oxide, liquid, at plant' as a real 8-input unit process. The initiator alcohol cannot be modelled: it is glycerol, sucrose or a glycol depending on the grade, the field does not say which, and BAFU-2026 has no petrochemical glycerol dataset at all - the only glycerine datasets in it are biodiesel co-products from rape and vegetable oil, which would import an agricultural background that has nothing to do with this product, and one deprecated 'xx Glycerine, from epichlorohydrin'. So the whole 1.000 kg is charged to propylene oxide, and unlike every other stoichiometric amount in this batch that is an UPPER bound, not a lower one: the true propylene-oxide content is 1.000 kg minus the initiator, typically a few to ten per cent. It is fixed rather than fitted because there is no printed range to bound it with. The ethylene-oxide tip of the common grades is a second, unquantifiable gap.
