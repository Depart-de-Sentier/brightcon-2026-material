# Check: Acetone, liquid, at plant (f44d9ac3-f0cb-311d-bdba-3fd3946b573c)

target `bafu-2026` vs explicit model `f44d9ac3-f0cb-311d-bdba-3fd3946b573c-disagg` and hybrid `f44d9ac3-f0cb-311d-bdba-3fd3946b573c-hybrid`

**Strategy:** S3 — transcribed cumene requirement, mass-allocated between acetone and its phenol co-product, utilities from the BAFU chemicals template and calibrated. Labelled S3, not S1, although the cumene figure is a straight transcription - because the number that goes into the node is not the number the report prints, and that difference is the point of this spec. Chapter 3 of 2007 - LCI chemicals - Althaus.pdf, sec. 1.5.2, states: 'Wells (1999) indicates that 2300 kg of cumene are required to produce 1000 kg of acetone with a yield of 90%. The production of the co-product phenol is not included in this figure.' Those 2300 kg are the *unallocated* requirement - 120.19/58.08 = 2.07 kg of cumene per kg of acetone at full conversion, divided by the stated 90 % yield, is 2.30 kg exactly - so charging them all to the acetone gives the phenol a free ride, and the phenol is the heavier product of the two (1.62 kg per kg of acetone). This spec therefore applies mass allocation: Cumene cleavage: C6H5C(CH3)2OOH -> C6H5OH + CH3COCH3, i.e. one phenol (94.113) per acetone (58.080), so the printed 2.300 kg of cumene per kg of acetone produces 1.620 kg of phenol alongside it. Mass allocation between the two co-products gives acetone 58.080/(58.080+94.113) = 38.16 % of the cumene, i.e. 2.300 x 0.38162 = 0.87772 kg per kg of acetone. Both numbers in that product are printed: 2300 kg per 1000 kg is Wells (1999) as quoted by Althaus sec. 1.5.2, and the molar masses are standard. Molar masses from the IUPAC 2021 standard atomic weights: H 1.008, C 12.011, N 14.007, O 15.999, S 32.06, Cl 35.45, Na 22.990. A companion spec, specs/f44d9ac3-acetone-liquid-at-plant.draft.json, is the unallocated transcription produced by the LLM route, and the two are meant to be read together. Disclosure, because it matters for how much weight the agreement figures deserve: the unallocated spec was built and checked first, and its flow diff showed its cumulative fossil CO2 at 4.06 kg against the target's 1.79 kg, a factor 2.3 - which is what prompted writing the allocated variant. The allocation is a modelling decision argued from the reaction equation and stated here in full, not a bound tuned until the score improved; no amount in this spec is free except the utilities, which carry no allocation question. Known gaps: the sulphuric acid of the cleavage step, named by the chapter and never quantified; the oxygen/air of the oxidation; and the second route the chapter describes (dehydrogenation of isopropanol), which the chapter says the PlasticsEurope data does not include.
