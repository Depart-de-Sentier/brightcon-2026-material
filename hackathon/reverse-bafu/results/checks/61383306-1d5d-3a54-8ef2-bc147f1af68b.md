# Check: Styrene-acrylonitrile copolymer, SAN, at plant (61383306-1d5d-3a54-8ef2-bc147f1af68b)

target `bafu-2026` vs explicit model `61383306-1d5d-3a54-8ef2-bc147f1af68b-disagg` and hybrid `61383306-1d5d-3a54-8ef2-bc147f1af68b-hybrid`

**Strategy:** S3 — top-down model: the two monomers named by the product, split fitted under a 1 kg mass constraint, utilities calibrated. NOT S1. No report in the bundle prints an inventory. The dataset's ecoSpold technology field says 'production by different polymerization processes out of its different monomers' - it asserts that the monomers are the input list but does not name them; the product name does: styrene-acrylonitrile copolymer. Both monomers exist in BAFU, acrylonitrile as a real 9-input unit process ('Acrylonitrile from Sohio process, at plant'), styrene only as an unflagged eco-profile. Addition copolymerisation loses no mass, so the two add up to 1.000 kg, which is imposed with mass_sum; the split is nowhere quantified in the bundle and is therefore fitted, not measured - flagged low confidence. The same caveat as HIPS applies: a good inventory fit here would not prove the split is right.

AFTER THE FIT - READ THIS BEFORE USING THE AMOUNTS. The calibration was run and its result is structurally wrong, which is worth more than the spec was: the fit drove acrylonitrile to 1.2E-10 kg, i.e. zero, and put the whole kilogram on styrene - a styrene-acrylonitrile copolymer modelled as pure polystyrene. The inventory agreement it buys is poor anyway: 3 of 1668 determined flows within +-10 %, median |delta| 133 %. This is the identifiability trap the project's own calibration benchmark predicts (README sec. 6: 'a better inventory fit with the wrong structure'), caught here on real data rather than on a synthetic case. The mechanism is specific and repeatable: under a mass constraint, when one candidate monomer is another dataset of the same APME eco-profile family and another is an ecoinvent unit process, the fit will empty the ecoinvent one, because it drags in a full background of ~1670 elementary flows that the target's ~145-substance eco-profile simply does not have, and every one of them counts against the fit. And among same-family candidates the fit follows the waste-treatment exchanges, not the chemistry - which is why it prefers polybutadiene to styrene: 'Styrene, at plant' declares 0.5615 kg of hazardous waste to incineration per kg, 80x what its own polymer GPPS declares (0.0070 kg) and 250x its own feedstock benzene (0.0022 kg), so any node that links styrene inherits a waste chain the target does not have. Conclusion for a user: the node's *inventory* is a fair reconstruction of the target's flows, its *composition* is not a statement about the product, and the split must not be quoted. The amounts are left exactly as the fit produced them - no bound was moved after the score was seen.
