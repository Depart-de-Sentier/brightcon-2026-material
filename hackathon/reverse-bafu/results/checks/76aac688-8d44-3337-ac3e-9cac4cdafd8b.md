# Check: Styrene, at plant (76aac688-8d44-3337-ac3e-9cac4cdafd8b)

target `bafu-2026` vs explicit model `76aac688-8d44-3337-ac3e-9cac4cdafd8b-disagg` and hybrid `76aac688-8d44-3337-ac3e-9cac4cdafd8b-hybrid`

**Strategy:** S3 — top-down model: ethylbenzene by the dehydrogenation equation with molar masses, utilities from the BAFU chemicals template and calibrated. NOT S1. No report in the bundle prints an inventory. The input list comes from the dataset's own ecoSpold technology field, 'mixture of process of dehydrogenation of ethyl benzene and oxirane process', and the amount from the dehydrogenation equation C6H5C2H5 -> C6H5CH=CH2 + H2: 106.168/104.152 = 1.01936 kg of ethylbenzene per kg of styrene at full conversion, with 0.01936 kg of hydrogen released. That is the stoichiometric floor - industrial reactors run 60-70 % conversion per pass with recycle, so the gross feed is higher but the net consumption per kg of product is this - and it is fixed, not fitted. The second route the field names, the oxirane (propylene-oxide/styrene-monomer) process, is not modelled: it is a two-product process whose share and allocation neither the field nor any bundled report gives, and folding it in would require inventing both. Said plainly: this model is the dehydrogenation route only. Dehydrogenation is strongly endothermic - about 1.2 MJ per kg of styrene of reaction enthalpy alone, before the steam dilution the process runs on - so the template's 2.0 MJ is certainly too low, which is precisely why heat and electricity are left free. The reason to do this one first: styrene is the monomer of GPPS, HIPS, SAN, ABS and the already-committed expandable-polystyrene spec, and 'Ethyl benzene, at plant' is a real 7-input BAFU unit process, so the chain terminates on the database. One level further down, ethylbenzene's own benzene input is itself an unflagged eco-profile whose rebuild is blocked by the BTX allocation - that is a finding about the database, not something this spec can fix.
