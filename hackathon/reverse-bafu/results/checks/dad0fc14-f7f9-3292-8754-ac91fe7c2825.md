# Check: Acrylonitrile-butadiene-styrene copolymer, ABS, at plant (dad0fc14-f7f9-3292-8754-ac91fe7c2825)

target `bafu-2026` vs explicit model `dad0fc14-f7f9-3292-8754-ac91fe7c2825-disagg` and hybrid `dad0fc14-f7f9-3292-8754-ac91fe7c2825-hybrid`

**Strategy:** S3 — top-down model: the three monomers the dataset names, split fitted under a 1 kg mass constraint, utilities calibrated. NOT S1. No report in the bundle prints an inventory. The dataset's ecoSpold technology field - 'production by emulsion polymerization out of its three monomers' - states the number of monomers, and the product name states which three: acrylonitrile, butadiene and styrene. That is the structure. No composition is printed anywhere in the bundle (the 2025 plastics report lists the 2015 PlasticsEurope ABS eco-profile in Tab. 6.1 and prints no inventory for it), so all three are free under mass_sum = 1.0 kg and the split is a fit, not a measurement - flagged low confidence. Two of the three monomers are themselves unflagged eco-profiles, so this model terminates on aggregated data and the structural check says so.

AFTER THE FIT - READ THIS BEFORE USING THE AMOUNTS. The calibration was run and its result is structurally wrong, which is worth more than the spec was: the fit drove acrylonitrile - the monomer the product is named after - to 8.9E-10 kg, i.e. zero, and split the kilogram as 0.601 kg butadiene / 0.391 kg styrene, where a real ABS is roughly 20 % acrylonitrile, 20 % butadiene and 60 % styrene. The inventory agreement it buys is the best in the whole family: 1221 of 1667 determined flows within +-10 % (73 %), median |delta| 6.7 %. This is the identifiability trap the project's own calibration benchmark predicts (README sec. 6: 'a better inventory fit with the wrong structure'), caught here on real data rather than on a synthetic case. The mechanism is specific and repeatable: under a mass constraint, when one candidate monomer is another dataset of the same APME eco-profile family and another is an ecoinvent unit process, the fit will empty the ecoinvent one, because it drags in a full background of ~1670 elementary flows that the target's ~145-substance eco-profile simply does not have, and every one of them counts against the fit. And among same-family candidates the fit follows the waste-treatment exchanges, not the chemistry - which is why it prefers polybutadiene to styrene: 'Styrene, at plant' declares 0.5615 kg of hazardous waste to incineration per kg, 80x what its own polymer GPPS declares (0.0070 kg) and 250x its own feedstock benzene (0.0022 kg), so any node that links styrene inherits a waste chain the target does not have. Conclusion for a user: the node's *inventory* is a fair reconstruction of the target's flows, its *composition* is not a statement about the product, and the split must not be quoted. The amounts are left exactly as the fit produced them - no bound was moved after the score was seen.
