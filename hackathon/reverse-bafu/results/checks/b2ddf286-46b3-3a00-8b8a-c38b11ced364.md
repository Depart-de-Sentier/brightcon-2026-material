# Check: Polystyrene, high impact, HIPS, at plant (b2ddf286-46b3-3a00-8b8a-c38b11ced364)

target `bafu-2026` vs explicit model `b2ddf286-46b3-3a00-8b8a-c38b11ced364-disagg` and hybrid `b2ddf286-46b3-3a00-8b8a-c38b11ced364-hybrid`

**Strategy:** S3 — top-down model: the two monomers named by the product, split fitted under a 1 kg mass constraint, utilities calibrated. NOT S1. No report in the bundle prints an inventory for this dataset. The dataset's own ecoSpold technology field is 'polymerization out of ethylene and benzene by free radical processes' - word for word the same text as the general-purpose grade, so the field does not distinguish HIPS from GPPS at all and gives no rubber. What does distinguish them is the product name: high-impact polystyrene is polystyrene toughened with polybutadiene rubber, which is why BAFU carries a separate dataset for it and why PlasticsEurope's own 2012 eco-profile for it is titled 'High impact polystyrene (HIPS); polymerisation of polybutadiene' (quoted in Tab. 6.1 of 2025 - LCI plastics - Rajabihamedani.pdf, which lists it as 'Recommended to be added' and prints no inventory). So the structure - styrene plus polybutadiene - is evidence-backed; the split is not. No number for the rubber content exists in the bundle, so both monomers are free and constrained only by mass_sum = 1.0 kg: addition polymerisation loses no mass, so whatever is not styrene is rubber. The split is therefore a fit, not a measurement, and is flagged low confidence. This is the same device the committed cement ZN spec uses for a composition given only as ranges.

AFTER THE FIT - READ THIS BEFORE USING THE AMOUNTS. The calibration was run and its result is structurally wrong, which is worth more than the spec was: the fit put 0.9998 kg on polybutadiene and 1.0E-4 kg on styrene - high-impact *polystyrene* modelled as pure rubber, where the real rubber content is a few per cent. The inventory agreement it buys is 621 of 1668 determined flows within +-10 % (37 %), median |delta| 11.3 %. This is the identifiability trap the project's own calibration benchmark predicts (README sec. 6: 'a better inventory fit with the wrong structure'), caught here on real data rather than on a synthetic case. The mechanism is specific and repeatable: under a mass constraint, when one candidate monomer is another dataset of the same APME eco-profile family and another is an ecoinvent unit process, the fit will empty the ecoinvent one, because it drags in a full background of ~1670 elementary flows that the target's ~145-substance eco-profile simply does not have, and every one of them counts against the fit. And among same-family candidates the fit follows the waste-treatment exchanges, not the chemistry - which is why it prefers polybutadiene to styrene: 'Styrene, at plant' declares 0.5615 kg of hazardous waste to incineration per kg, 80x what its own polymer GPPS declares (0.0070 kg) and 250x its own feedstock benzene (0.0022 kg), so any node that links styrene inherits a waste chain the target does not have. Conclusion for a user: the node's *inventory* is a fair reconstruction of the target's flows, its *composition* is not a statement about the product, and the split must not be quoted. The amounts are left exactly as the fit produced them - no bound was moved after the score was seen.
