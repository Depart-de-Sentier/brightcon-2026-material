5 · Step 2 — calibrate calibrate.py · lci.py

Only the top node is calibrated; nested nodes keep their spec amounts. This step is a bounded, weighted, linear least-squares problem in the amounts of the inputs marked free, solved twice. The structure — which inputs exist at all — is never a variable here, and §5.7 derives why.

5.1 · Notation

Everything lives in flow space: n = 1,927 elementary-flow rows of the EF 3.1 biosphere, no characterisation anywhere. lci.System.cumulative returns BA−1ej — the cumulative inventory of one activity — as a column.

  • bcumulative inventory of the target, the system process being rebuilt
  • Mfn×q; columns are the cumulative inventories of the q free inputs
  • Mxn×p; the same for the fixed inputs, held at their spec amounts ax
  • dthe node's own direct elementary flows, as a vector
  • athe unknown: amounts of the free inputs, bounded by lo ≤ a ≤ hi
  • m(a)the model inventory Mxax + d + Mfa
  • rthe residual target — what the free inputs have to explain
  • w, Wrow weights and W = diag(w); R = { i : wi > 0 }
  • κ, μindicator of free inputs measured in kilograms; the node's mass_sum
  • Λdiag of column norms, used to precondition the solve

5.2 · The residual least-squares problem

We want the model inventory to match the target, m(a) ≈ b. Everything that is not a free amount is known, so move it to the right-hand side — that subtraction is the whole of residual_target:

r  =  b  −  Mxax  −  d residual target
â  =  arg min lo ≤ a ≤ hi   ‖ W (Mfa − r) ‖22  +  106‖y‖2 (κ⊤a − μ)2

Written out over the rows that carry weight, with y = Wr restricted to R:

Σi∈R   wi2  [ m(a)i − bi ]2  +  106‖y‖2 (κ⊤a − μ)2

because (Mfa − r)i = m(a)i − bi identically. The residual of the fit is the flow-by-flow error of the rebuilt process against the original. The mass term is dropped when mass_sum is null; the weights enter squared, since the code scales the rows of both Mf and r by w and hands the result to a plain least-squares solver.

This is a convex quadratic on a box — a unique minimum in Mfa, though not necessarily in a: if two free inputs have near-parallel cumulative inventories, many a give the same fit. That degeneracy is the subject of §5.7.

5.3 · Why those weights

lci.relative_weights then lci.System.fit_weights build w in two moves. First, per flow, against a frozen model m̃ from the previous pass:

basei  =  1 / max( |bi|, |m̃i| )     (0 if both are 0)

The point of the max is asymmetry-avoidance: dividing by |bi| alone would be a division by zero for any flow the model invents that the target does not have, so such a flow would be free. Here it counts by its own size. Substituting into §5.2, each term becomes a squared relative deviation — a kilogram of CO₂ and a microgram of a dioxin are the same equation, and no characterisation factor is involved.

wi  =  basei  /  √ng(i)      so the objective is   Σg (1/ng) Σi∈g (relative deviationi)2

Second: the 29 groups g are the (unit, top-level compartment) pairs — kilogram/air (77 flows), square metre/land use (57), kilo Becquerel/water (51) — and ng is the number of weighted flows in a group. Each group contributes its mean squared relative deviation, so the 1,294 flows of kilogram/emissions do not outvote the 57 of square metre/land use. The flip side is that a group of one flow — cubic metre-year/land use is exactly that — speaks as loudly as the entire emissions block. Flows that the sparse solve only produces as round-off (lci.determined_flows) get weight 0: their relative deviation is noise.

What this replaced. Until 2026-09-23 the second move divided by Σj∈g basej, to make Σw = 1 per group. But that sum is dominated by the group's smallest flow, so each group's relative deviations were scaled by roughly its smallest amount. On a typical target, kilogram/emissions (CO₂ and ~1,200 others) weighed 10−58 of the single land-use flow, and the fit effectively saw about 20 of ~1,670 flows. On the calibration benchmark the change moves the complete-list row from 767 to 787 of 803 material amounts within ±20 %, and the no-list row from 64 % to 98 % of flows within ±10 %.

5.4 · The mass-sum row, and why 106

When a spec sets mass_sum — the composition must add to 1 kg of product — one row is appended to the already-weighted system, with coefficient κj = 1 for free inputs whose unit starts with kilo and weight 103‖y‖:

cost of missing the mass balance by Δ  =  106‖y‖2Δ2   vs.   whole flow-fit objective ≲ ‖y‖2

Taking a = 0 gives objective exactly ‖y‖2, so the optimum is no worse than that. Any Δ ≳ 10−3 therefore costs more than the entire rest of the fit is worth: the balance holds to about a part in 103 of μ. A penalty, not a hard constraint — but tight enough to act as one, and it keeps the problem a plain box-constrained least squares that NNLS or BVLS can take.

5.5 · Column scaling is an exact reparametrisation, not a fudge

The columns of the weighted design matrix A differ by ten orders of magnitude, because a unit of manufacturing plant and a kilogram of tap water are both legitimate inputs. Measured on the CH gypsum fibre board template's ten inputs, ‖c‖ runs from 1.05×1010 (wooden board manufacturing plant, unit) down to 0.12 (tap water, kilogram) — a spread of 8.7×1010. Left alone, a solver stalls. Scaling the columns is not enough for lsq_linear's default trust-region method either: it tests an absolute tolerance, and on the benchmark it stopped early in 14 of 100 complete-list cases, at an objective 102–1013 above that of the true amounts. So lci.solve_weighted scales the right-hand side too and uses an active-set method — NNLS when the only bound is a ≥ 0, BVLS otherwise. With Λ = diag(‖Aej‖2):

(AΛ−1)(Λa)  =  Aa    and    lo ≤ a ≤ hi  ⟺  Λlo ≤ Λa ≤ Λhi

Since every Λjj > 0, the substitution z = Λa is a bijection of the feasible box that leaves the objective value unchanged. The solved problem is therefore the same problem, not an approximation of it — only the conditioning differs, every column of AΛ−1 now having unit norm. That is why rescaling the solution at the end is exact rather than a correction.

5.6 · Two passes: truncated IRLS

The weights depend on the model, and the model depends on the amounts — so the honest problem, min a ‖W(m(a))(Mfa − r)‖2, is not linear. The code runs iteratively reweighted least squares and stops at two:

a0 = the spec's amounts   →   a1 = solve with W(m(a0))   →   a2 = solve with W(m(a1))   →   return a2

Two is a truncation chosen empirically, not a convergence result — there is no fixed-point test in the code, and the dependence on a draft's starting amounts is attenuated rather than removed: a0 still reaches the answer through W(m(a1)). It is weak because the weight saturates: basei = 1/max(|bi|, |m̃i|) is flat in m̃ wherever the model is below the target, so every flow the draft did not wildly overshoot contributes the identical weight in both passes.

5.7 · Why the input list is never a variable

This is the mathematical core of the whole method, and it follows from one identity. Scale production to 1 on the diagonal and write A = I − T, with Tkj ≥ 0 the amount of k consumed per unit of j. Since (I−T)−1 = I + T + T2 + …, the cumulative inventory cj = BA−1ej of every activity satisfies

cj  =  dj  +  Σk Tkj ck Leontief

where dj = Bej is the activity's direct flows alone. In words: every candidate column is exactly a non-negative combination of its own suppliers' columns, plus its direct flows. Checked numerically on 60 random bafu-2026 activities — maximum relative error 1.1×10−8, median 3.6×10−11, which is LU round-off. It is an identity, not an approximation.

Now the damage. dj is small: over those 60 activities the median ‖dj‖/‖cj‖ is 1×10−4, and 80 % sit below 10−2 — most of a product's burden comes from upstream, not from its own gate. So each candidate column lies within a relative distance ‖dj‖/‖cj‖ of the cone spanned by other candidates. When the input list is free and the candidate set is drawn from one database, those suppliers are in the set, and the objective of §5.2 has directions along which it changes by almost nothing. The minimiser is then picked by round-off and by which columns happen to be present — not by evidence.

Stucco, at plant [CH]:   ‖d‖/‖c‖ = 6.6×10−3

So 99.3 % of stucco's cumulative inventory is reproduced exactly by 1.16 kg gypsum (at mine) + 0.70 MJ natural-gas heat + 0.175 MJ heavy-fuel-oil heat + 0.027 kWh electricity + 1.4×10−4 industrial machine. That is the arithmetic behind the failure reported in §2 of the concept page: given a free structure, the solver reproduced a known process's whole cumulative inventory while choosing 116 inputs, of which 2 had the right amount, and turned 0.71 kg of stucco into 4.7 kg of lime plus 0.8 kg of anhydrite. It did not malfunction — it walked down a flat direction that the supply chain guarantees exists.

Fixing the list removes the cone. With q ≈ 10 named columns chosen from evidence rather than 2,965 drawn from a database, the substitutions above are no longer available, and the amounts come back to ~10 %. Bounds narrow what is left. This is why §5 fits amounts and never names, and why the benchmark's no‑list rows are the numbers that matter: offered every dataset in the database and no list (blind‑all), the fit reproduces 100 % of the flows within ±10 % with a process of ~71 wrong inputs.

5.8 · Amounts that land on a bound

At the optimum the KKT conditions on the box read, with gradient ∇ = 2(Mf)⊤W2(Mfâ − r):

loj < âj < hij ⟹ ∇j = 0   ·   âj = loj ⟹ ∇j ≥ 0   ·   âj = hij ⟹ ∇j ≤ 0

An amount pinned at a bound means the unconstrained best fit lies outside the range the report gives — the gradient is still pushing outward when it hits the wall. That is a substantive finding, not a numerical artefact: the report and the original dataset disagree. The harness prints these, and the inventories page tags them "calibrated · at upper bound". Three of gypsum fibre board's six fitted amounts sit on the 2× ceiling, which is the honest statement that the Swiss template does not describe the German plant.

5.9 · Three different things are called "residual"

NameDefinitionWhere
residual targetr = b − Mxax − d§5.2 — what the free inputs must explain; the RHS of the fit
fit residualMfâ − r = m(â) − b§5.2 — what the fit could not explain; its weighted norm is the residual norm printed by calibrate
build residualcum(target) − cum(explicit)§6 — carried as direct biosphere flows on the -hybrid node

The last two are the same quantity with opposite sign. After calibrate --apply, the explicit node is the model, cum(explicit) = m(â), so the build residual equals −(fit residual) exactly — for a flat spec. With nested nodes the two part company, because calibrate only ever touches the top node's free inputs while build walks the whole tree.

    print "nothing to calibrate", return (this is S1 with a complete table)
  • any input has "sandbox"

  • always
    residual target r = b − Mˣ·aˣ − d; one row per elementary flow, weighted 1/max(|target|, |model|) — relative error, no characterisation
  • row weights
    wᵢ = 1 / max(|bᵢ|, |mᵢ|), where m is the model: every flow present in either inventory is one equation in relative error, so a kilogram of CO₂ and a microgram of a dioxin count alike and a flow the model invents counts by its own size. Then divided by √(flows in its (unit, compartment) group) — kilogram/air, square metre/land use, kilo Becquerel/water, 29 of them — so every group contributes its mean squared relative error and the 1,294 flows of kilogram/emissions do not outvote the rest; round-off flows (lci.determined_flows) get weight 0.   • input.bounds given

  • always

7 · Step 4 — check check.py

Inputs: target, explicit node, hybrid node if present. One System with demand on the explicit node; three cumulative inventories in one solve.

  • flow agreement
    per elementary flow of the target: Δ = explicit/target − 1, bucketed (≤ 10 %, 10–20 %, 20–50 %, 50–100 %, > 100 %, missing); three headline numbers — the 50 largest kilogram flows within ±10 %, the share of the target's kilogram mass within ±10 %, and all flows within ±10 %
  • largest flows
    per unit (kg, MJ, kBq, m³ …) the largest flows of the target with their explicit value, Δ and residual; then the 10 worst deviations among the 50 largest kilogram flows — the diagnostic that turns "radon is −51 %" into "the grid electricity is too low"
  • hybrid present

  • structural check 1

  • structural check 2

  • structural check 3
