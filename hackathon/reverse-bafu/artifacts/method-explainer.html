Brightcon 2026 hackathon · issue #38 · reverse-bafu

Dis‑aggregating system processes into unit processes

The Swiss BAFU‑2026 database ships at least 138 datasets as system processes: the result of a supply chain without the chain. This page explains what they are, why they cannot simply be “fitted” back into shape, and the algorithm we built to dis‑aggregate them from evidence — with the checks that tell us when it worked.

1 · The problem: two ways to store the same product

An LCA database describes products as unit processes: one production step, its direct emissions, and links to the products it consumes. Impacts are computed by following the links — the supply chain is resolved at calculation time, which is why you can swap a supplier, change a region, or ask where an impact comes from.

A system process (ecoSpold type=2, “system terminated”) stores the result of that resolution instead: every upstream emission and resource, already multiplied through, as one long list of elementary flows and no links at all. It scores correctly and explains nothing.

BAFU‑2026 v1 contains 11,947 datasets. 101 carry the type=2 flag; they come from five sources: PlasticsEurope eco‑profiles (14, consumed by ~1,050 other datasets), confidential ecoinvent‑v2‑era industry data (24), treeze/KBOB reports (10), French bio‑based construction studies (29), and manufacturer‑specific KBOB entries (24). 37 more carry no flag and were found by structure — no production input, only waste‑treatment services, a full flow vector: the APME‑era eco‑profiles of benzene, styrene, the nylons, polycarbonate and others. 138 in all. What each report actually prints and who entered the data are on the companion page BAFU System Processes. The hackathon question: can they be replaced by unit processes, with results unchanged — and how much of that can be automated?

2 · The experiment that shaped the method

The tempting shortcut is to treat it as a fitting problem: the aggregated flow list is a target vector, every dataset in the database has a known cumulative inventory, so solve for the mix of inputs that reproduces the target. We tried it on a dataset whose true inputs we knew.

Gypsum fibre board (CH), 10 real inputs. Given 2,965 candidate inputs including all ten correct ones, non‑negative least squares reproduced the whole cumulative inventory — and chose 116 inputs, of which 2 had the right amount. The 0.71 kg of stucco became 4.7 kg of lime plus 0.8 kg of anhydrite: the solver replaced the input by its own supply chain. Given only the ten correct names, the same solver recovered 8 of 10 amounts within ±12 %.

The reason is structural. In a Leontief system every input's inventory is a linear combination of its suppliers' inventories, so columns are nearly collinear and a cumulative flow vector is compatible with a large family of different processes. Two consequences drive everything below:

Inventory agreement is necessary, not sufficient. “Results stay the same” cannot be the proof that a rebuild is right — and that holds a fortiori for impact scores, which aggregate thousands of flows into 25 numbers and hide even more. Everything below is therefore judged on the elementary flows themselves, never on characterised results.
The input list must come from evidence. Once the names are fixed, the amounts are recoverable; without them the fit invents a process.

3 · The algorithm

One system process at a time. The unit of work is a spec: a JSON file holding the evidence‑derived unit process — where each number came from, which inputs are certain, which may move within a range. Four steps run on it.

  1. Resolve — terminate on the database reverse-bafu resolveEvery input name in the spec is matched to a BAFU dataset by name and location. Each lands in one of three states: unit (an existing unit process — link and stop), aggregated (the only match is itself a system process — link, but flag the dependency so it gets rebuilt first), or missing (nothing in BAFU — the spec needs a nested new node, or a better name; close matches are suggested). Direct emissions and resources resolve against the EF 3.1 biosphere the same way. Units are checked: the gypsum board case was caught here because the target is per m² and the template per kg.
  2. Calibrate — amounts for the inputs marked free, list fixed reverse-bafu calibrateOnly inputs the evidence leaves uncertain are marked free, optionally with bounds from the report's ranges and a mass_sum when the composition must add up. Everything else is held at its transcribed amount and subtracted from the target first. Then bounded least squares fits the free amounts against the aggregated flow vector — one equation per elementary flow in relative error, with each (unit, compartment) group of flows counting equally, solved on a rescaled system with an exact active‑set method (NNLS, or BVLS when there are bounds). No characterisation factors enter at any point. The structure never changes here — that is the lesson of section 2. When an amount lands on a bound, the report's range disagrees with the original data; the harness shows it.
  3. Build — explicit node and hybrid node reverse-bafu buildThe rebuilt process is written into a sandbox database linked to BAFU, never over the original. Two versions: the explicit node is the model; the hybrid node is the same plus a block of residual elementary flows equal to original − Σ inputs' inventories, so its cumulative inventory equals the original exactly. The hybrid makes “results stay the same” true by construction; the residual's share per category measures how much is still unexplained; negative residual entries flag over‑explained inputs.
  4. Check — the harness reverse-bafu checkThe rebuilt process is compared with the original flow by flow — no characterisation method, no impact categories, so no weighting policy can flatter the result. Three headline numbers: how many of the 50 largest kilogram flows land within ±10 %, how much of the original's total kilogram mass does, and how many of all its flows do (the last is dominated by the long tail of trace flows that only a full background chain carries). Per unit, the largest flows are listed with their deviation — the diagnostic that turns a number into “the grid electricity is 28× too high”. And structural checks that a score can't give: a plausible number of inputs, no grid mix decomposed into power plants, no dependency on another system process, mass in vs out, everything resolved.

4 · How deep to rebuild: terminate on the database

Rebuilding means writing unit processes top‑down. At every input the question is: does BAFU already have a unit process for this? If yes, link and stop. If not, write a new intermediate node and ask again one level down. Stop as soon as every leaf is an existing dataset.

HDPE granulate (new node) ├─ electricity, medium voltage → exists in BAFU link, stop ├─ steam / heat, natural gas → exists link, stop ├─ chemical plant, organics → exists link, stop ├─ disposal services → exist link, stop └─ ethylene → only a system process └─ steam cracking (new node, multi‑output) ├─ naphtha, at refinery → exists (32‑input unit process) link, stop ├─ heat / electricity → exist link, stop └─ cracker plant → no node → new leaf, or approximate

Two new nodes, and the chain terminates. Rebuilding shallower would leave a system process one level down (ethylene is itself one); rebuilding deeper would duplicate naphtha and crude‑oil datasets BAFU already models well — and produce a different naphtha from the rest of the database. “Exists” means exists as a unit process: an aggregated match does not terminate, it orders the work (ethylene before HDPE, scutching before flax insulation). New nodes are shared: one cracker serves six polymers, one scutching node ~10 bio‑based datasets.

5 · Strategies, by the evidence available

The algorithm is the same for every dataset; what differs is where the input list comes from. Three routes, from strongest evidence to weakest:

Fitting the flow vector alone is not a route: it is only the amount step inside S1–S3 (section 2). And every rebuild, whatever its route, also gets the hybrid node of step 3: explicit inputs plus a residual block, so it reproduces the original exactly while the explicit part improves.

7 · Testing the algorithm on cases with a known answer

On the real system processes we can never know whether a rebuilt structure is right — only whether the flows match, and section 2 shows that proves little. So we test on synthetic system processes: take a BAFU unit process, compute its cumulative inventory (exactly what a type=2 export of it would contain), hand the algorithm that vector plus an evidence package of controlled quality, and compare with the real inputs.

Test set: 100 unit processes, stratified over BAFU categories, 3–30 inputs each, seeded. Evidence packages: oracle (correct input list, no amounts), bounded (list + amounts known to a factor 2, like report ranges), partial (the 30 % least important inputs missing, like a report that omits minor lines), distractors (list + 10 plausible wrong candidates, like an LLM over‑proposing), and two controls with no list at all: blind (the ~675 datasets used at least 30 times in BAFU as candidates) and blind‑all (all ~12,000 datasets as candidates).

Reading the table: given the right names, the amounts come back (rows 1–2); the few misses are pairs of inputs whose cumulative inventories are near‑identical, such as inert waste and gravel to the same landfill, where any split gives the same flows. A report that omits minor lines still matches the flows, but the remaining amounts shift to cover for the missing ones. Ten wrong candidates are set to zero — an optimistic result, because a synthetic target is reproduced exactly by its true inputs, while a real original, computed on older background data, leaves a mismatch that a wrong candidate can absorb. And the two blind rows are the identifiability trap measured: with no list, the fit reproduces 97–100 % of the flows with a process of 71–96 wrong inputs. That is the number behind “evidence before amounts”: a good flow match does not show that the inputs are right.

“Material” inputs are those whose true contribution reaches 1 % of the target amount of some flow the calculation determines (round‑off flows are left out, as in the flow score). Building the benchmark also exposed numerical traps, now fixed in the pipeline: columns spanning 10¹² in magnitude (a “unit” of factory vs a kg of gravel) stall a solver unless scaled, and even scaled, lsq_linear's trust‑region method stops early on its absolute tolerance, so the fit uses an active‑set method. And normalising each group of flows by its sum of weights, as an earlier version did, weights a group by its smallest flow — it silenced the kilogram emissions; each group now contributes its mean squared relative error.

8 · Where LLMs fit — and where they don't

Good fit

  • Extracting inventory tables and parameters from the report PDFs (114 ship with BAFU; file names match the XML source titles)
  • Mapping report line items to BAFU dataset names — 11,947 candidates, naming conventions, locations
  • Reading the ecoSpold comments (includedProcesses, technology) into a candidate input list
  • Drafting the spec and its evidence notes; reading the flow diff and proposing the next edit

Not a fit

  • Choosing amounts without a fixed structure — that is section 2 again
  • Deciding allocation for multi‑output nodes (cracker, scutching)
  • Signing off a rebuilt process: the structural checks need a practitioner's eye
  • Guessing what a report left out; the residual and flow diff are the honest answer there

This is now a pipeline of its own, reverse-bafu draft-all, run with a pinned model or, via /draft-all, a Claude Code session. A first prompt locates the inventory table in the cited report; the pages and the dataset's metadata are extracted deterministically (hashed); two fixed prompts follow — extraction with verbatim quotes, then mapping against deterministically generated candidate lists. Where no report prints the inventory, a template route (a same‑product unit process) and a metadata route (the dataset's own description and stoichiometry) take over. Every route ends in a spec in which every number carries a derivation: the quoted line, the raw value, the conversion factor with its source, the chosen dataset and why, the author, and a reviewed_by field. The prompt forbids the two things that need judgement — inventing inputs the excerpt does not mention (they go into gaps) and settling amounts it is unsure of (flagged for calibration). On burnt shale the drafted spec equalled the interactive one on every transcribed number; 44 of the 51 rebuilds were written in interactive LLM sessions before these routes existed, 7 by the routes themselves. The metric the hackathon can report: per evidence tier, the share of datasets that pass the harness without a human edit.

9 · Limits and open questions

51 of the 138 rebuilt: 32 completely opened, 19 still linking to a system process we did not rebuild. For the other 87 we found too little information to give the fit a chance. The PlasticsEurope polymers were rebuilt from their monomer and a generic chemicals block; the steam‑cracker sub‑model that would open the chain one level deeper is still a modelling task, not a fitting one. The 29 French datasets are blocked on reports held by their author (HEIG‑VD). The twelve worst matches are APME eco‑profiles that declare only ~130 flows themselves; the other ~1,500 reach them only as traces through waste links, which no rebuild on ecoinvent chemicals can reproduce. And the residual is carried as proxy flows, in the spirit of Schindler et al. 2025, in the hybrid node.
