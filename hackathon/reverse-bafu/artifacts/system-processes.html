The system processes of BAFU‑2026 and what stands behind them

Which datasets ship as system processes, where they came from, what their reports actually print, who entered them, and how many of them we dis‑aggregated. Data basis: results/system_terminated.csv (the 101 with the ecoSpold flag), results/system_terminated_extended.csv (all 138), the raw ecoSpold XML, the 114 report PDFs of the BAFU documentation bundle, and the bafu‑2026 Brightway database.

1 · What they are

We found 138 system processes. 101 carry ecoSpold dataSetInformation@type = 2: the dataset holds the cumulative elementary flows of its whole upstream chain — an LCI result — not the process's own flows plus links to suppliers. 87 have no technosphere inputs at all; the 14 PlasticsEurope polymers keep 1–17 inputs, but those are only the waste‑treatment services ecoinvent v2 attached to the eco‑profile's reported waste amounts. The production chain itself (crude oil → naphtha → cracker → polymer) is folded into the flows.

The long flow lists are real, not padded: the ~1,300‑flow (Empa) and ~1,660‑flow (French) vectors have 0–2 zero entries each. They are the full ecoinvent‑v2 / openLCA background, resolved. A 102nd flagged dataset (Disposal, rectangular straw bale) has no exchanges at all and is dropped.

The flag is reliable but not complete. 37 more carry no flag and were found by structure (scripts/find_system_processes.py): no production input at all, only waste‑treatment services, but a full elementary‑flow vector. They are the APME / PlasticsEurope‑era eco‑profiles — benzene, toluene, styrene, the nylons, ABS, polycarbonate, MDI, TDI, the PMMAs and others — and their own metadata confirms it ("Aggregated data for all processes from raw material extraction until delivery at plant"). 466 BAFU‑2026 datasets consume at least one of them. The sections below survey the 101 family by family; the 37 are covered in exports/README.md.

4 · What the ecoSpold metadata adds — and who to ask

Beyond the source citation, every dataset carries the data‑entry person, the data‑generator person and the proof‑reading validator (name, company code, country; addresses and e‑mails anonymised), a representativeness block (sampling procedure, percent of production covered, production volume, extrapolations), a time period, and sometimes a second source. These are columns in results/system_terminated.csv.

Two things follow. The French reports were brought into BAFU by Sébastien Lasvaux (HEIG‑VD, Yverdon), so they are to be requested from him, not searched for. And the representativeness fields give the plastics fit a hard constraint: the eco‑profile is a production‑weighted European average, not a single site.

What the metadata does not add: the per‑exchange comments on aggregated datasets are pedigree placeholders only — (5,5,5,5,5,BU:x); All uncertainty‑values set to 5.0 by default as data comes from System process — so there is no per‑flow provenance to mine. The default‑5 pedigree is itself a usable aggregation marker, complementary to type = 2.

6 · Open questions

  • The 2022 French rapports méthodologiques: request from Sébastien Lasvaux (HEIG‑VD).
  • Tolerance on the inventory itself: the rebuilt process is compared flow by flow with the original, so no characterisation method or weighting between impact categories enters the verdict. ±10 % on the large flows is a starting proposal; the long tail of trace flows from background chains will not be reproduced by a handful of explicit inputs for method reasons.
  • PlasticsEurope: rebuild against the 2012–2014 eco‑profiles BAFU holds (with the 2025 methane update) or the current versions?
  • The calibration uses no impact assessment and so needs no category weighting: flows are fitted in relative error, with each (unit, compartment) group of flows counting equally. What still has to be agreed is the tolerance per category used to judge a rebuild, not a weighting used to produce one.
  • Report the dropped type flag to sentier‑importers so the list becomes a column upstream.
