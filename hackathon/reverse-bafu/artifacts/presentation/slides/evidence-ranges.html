S3 · the input list is the report's; the composition is calibrated inside the printed ranges

Tschümperlin (2020), Ökobilanz ausgewählter Betonsorten, p. 21 (PDF p. 25), Tab. 3.14 · specs/c3490cfc-cement-zn-d.json

At first sight this is a complete inventory: the dataset's own table in the concrete report. But look at the top half. The energy, the grinding aid, the plant, the steel and both transports each have one number, and our rebuild matches every one of them exactly. The composition, which is what a kilogram of this cement is actually made of, is only given as ranges and upper limits: clinker forty to seventy percent, and so on. So the list is the report's and seven amounts are the report's, but the six shares that carry nearly all of the mass come from the optimiser. The table on the right shows what the optimiser did with that freedom: three of the six shares finished on an edge of their range, pushing against the report's limit. Where that happens, the data wanted to go further than the report allows. So this is S3: the input list is the report's, and the amounts that carry the mass are calibrated inside the ranges it prints. We used to count it as S1 because the ranges are printed.
