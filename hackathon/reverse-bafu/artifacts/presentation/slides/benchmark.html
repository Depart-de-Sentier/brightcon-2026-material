This is how we know the method works, and where it stops working. We take a hundred real unit processes, aggregate them the same way BAFU does, throw the input list away, and hand the algorithm a candidate input list of deliberately varying quality. Every panel is a case where the fit has to set the amounts, which is exactly the S2 and S3 situation; an S1 report needs no fit at all. In none of the panels does the fit get the input amounts: it gets an input list, and in the first panel a range of a factor of two either way for each amount, and has to find the amounts itself. Where a report prints an amount, the real pipeline never fits it; that input is held fixed. How to read a panel: every dot is one input of one case, its true amount across and its fitted amount up, so on the diagonal means recovered. The bottom strip holds true inputs the rebuild lost, fitted to zero or never on the list; the left strip holds wrong inputs the fit gave a real amount. The axes span almost thirty orders of magnitude, a unit of plant next to kilograms of material, so a miss of fifty percent still looks like a hit; that is why the count within twenty percent is written under each panel. The two scores: the median share of a case's elementary flows that land within ten percent of the original, and, summed over all cases, how many input amounts land within twenty percent of the true value, counting only inputs that contribute at least one percent to some flow the calculation determines. The panels run from the most constrained problem to the least. Given the complete list with every amount known to within a factor of two, every elementary flow lands within ten percent and 780 of 786 input amounts within twenty percent. Given the complete list and no amounts at all, it is still every flow and 772 of 786 input amounts. The fourteen that miss are pairs of inputs that the elementary flows cannot tell apart, such as concrete gravel and unreinforced concrete at the same sorting plant, or zinc coating of pieces and of coils: any split between the two gives the same flows, which is what the ranges settle. A list padded with ten plausible but wrong processes does no harm in this test: the fit sets all ten to zero. That result is optimistic, and worth saying out loud. Here the true inputs reproduce the target exactly, so a wrong candidate has nothing left to explain. The real originals were computed on older background data, and on real data a wrong candidate can absorb that mismatch; this drift is not tested here. The last panel is the important one. With no list at all and every one of the almost twelve thousand datasets in the database on offer, the fit reproduces the elementary flows within ten percent, a median of one hundred percent and every single flow in sixty of the hundred cases, with a process it made up: a median of 71 inputs that are not in the real one, and only 149 of 786 true input amounts right. It picks look-alikes: Libyan crude oil rebuilt as seventy-one percent Algerian and twenty-nine percent Egyptian crude, hard-coal electricity rebuilt from coal heat plus electricity from waste incineration. With only the 675 most used processes on offer it was ninety-seven percent of the flows and 264 of 599 amounts: more candidates make the flow match better and the inputs worse. With this many candidates the answer is not even unique; two runs of the same fit can land on different, equally good processes. So a good match of the elementary flows says nothing about whether the inputs are right. The fit's job is the input amounts; the inputs come from evidence. If someone asks about the fit itself: every elementary flow is one equation, weighted by its relative error, and each group of elementary flows, land use, water, radioactivity, each emission compartment, counts equally; the problem is badly scaled, a unit of plant next to a kilogram of material, so it is solved with an exact non-negative least-squares method on a rescaled system. An earlier version of the fit did neither well, and read far worse on this table; the numbers here are from the corrected fit, which the real rebuilds use too. A note on the hundred percent: we only score the elementary flows the calculation actually determines. About a hundred and twenty of the eighteen hundred elementary flows per process come out of the sparse solve as round-off, so they carry no information and are excluded and counted separately. Genuine trace emissions like dioxins stay in.
