Fifty-one of the hundred and thirty-eight we found, taken all the way through, and the split is the first point. Only one is a true transcription, burnt shale, where the report prints every amount. Eleven reuse the structure of a unit process BAFU already has for the same product. Thirty-nine, more than three quarters, are top-down models: the evidence names the inputs, not every amount. The plot on the right is every elementary flow of all fifty-one rebuilds against the original, about eighty-five thousand of them. They follow the line, but loosely: of the elementary flows both sides have, twenty-three percent match within ten percent and forty-three percent within a factor of two, and the largest flows match no better than the trace flows. On climate change, EF 3.1, the rebuilt process is within ten percent of the original for eleven of the fifty-one and within twenty-five percent for twenty-six. The median deviation per route is minus one percent for the transcription, minus four percent for the template transfers and minus fifteen percent for the top-down models, so the top-down models run about fifteen percent low, most likely because the fit pushes the energy inputs the reports never quantify towards zero. The fit itself uses no impact assessment; the climate score is only how we report the result, and it is in results/climate_comparison.csv. Where it differs from fossil carbon dioxide alone, another greenhouse gas decides it: Nylon 66 is forty-six percent low on fossil carbon dioxide but eighty-six percent high on climate change. Part of the gap is not ours: the originals were computed on older background data, and even BAFU's own dis-aggregated epoxy misses its aggregate's climate change score by a factor of two point seven. The box under the table: thirty-two are opened completely, down to existing unit processes, some of them through our own rebuilds; nineteen still link to one or more system processes we did not resolve. Eight datasets cause all of it, and Ethylene, average, at plant alone blocks nine.
